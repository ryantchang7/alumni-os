01 · Who and what

A rostered player who shipped a real application

Ryan Chang, current Penn men's golf student-athlete. Over roughly the past year he single-handedly designed, built, shipped and filmed penngolfclubhouse.com, a private site for Penn Men's Golf alumni and current players.

He is a student developer rather than a professional engineer, and built this with heavy AI assistance. That bears on long-term maintainability, not on whether it works. It demonstrably works.

The clock

His standing as a current player is the thing that gets him in a room at all. A rostered athlete walking into an Athletics office is a different conversation from an alum emailing in, and it expires at graduation. Every recommendation here is shaped by that.

What it does

A Member Book registry across generations, and a claim flow where alumni claim their own roster entry subject to approval. The Course runs organized rounds with tee sheets and group assignment; The 19th Hole does the same for coffee, drinks and dinners; Open Requests covers "I'm in town." Then Moments (a photo and video wall), Locker Room (a players-and-alumni-only tier), Career Room (find members by industry), and Ask (a current player requests an introduction to an alum). Plus Team Room and The Season, direct chat, a member map, Hall of Fame, and around 25 internal routes for roster scraping, data quality, the claims queue and launch readiness.

The film

Public at /launch. Probed from the file actually served: 4 minutes 51 seconds, 1920 by 1080, 53 MB. A 64 second cut also exists in 1080p and 4K. Neither has a music bed; that decision was never finalized.

04 · The constraint everything else follows from

340 names. Three ways to reach anyone.

340In the Member Book
3Email addresses
1LinkedIn URLs
0Phone numbers

You know who every Penn golfer since 1930 is. You cannot contact almost any of them. The archive was built from public pages, and public pages do not list email addresses.

The launch runbook's "send the alumni email to 340 people" step has no list behind it. It cannot happen as written. A launch through people you personally know still works, and its realistic ceiling is 20 to 40 people, nearly all from the 2020s.

This is why the advice from earlier flips. Penn is not an accelerant for reaching the other 330. It is the only channel. That argues for going sooner, not for waiting to build traction you have no way to build.

05 · The opening

Scotland, and it was on your site the whole time

46 days until the St Andrews Links Collegiate
Tournament 12 to 14 October. Alumni tour 14 to 17 October.

Penn competes against Texas, Washington and St Andrews, men's and women's teams both, two rounds on the Jubilee and match play on the Old Course. All three days televised on Golf Channel. Then the alumni tour: Old Course Hotel, the Castle Course, Kingsbarns, Carnoustie, dinner at Rusacks above the eighteenth.

Registration ran through 30 June at $5,000, $7,500 and $10,000, the upper tiers folding a tax-deductible gift into the Penn Golf annual fund. You told me registration has closed and treated that as a loss. It is the opposite.

What a closed registration actually means
  • The list already exists. People who paid five to ten thousand dollars to fly to Scotland with the team are, by revealed preference, the most committed alumni in the entire population. Someone has their contact details.
  • You will be standing next to them. Your own Scotland page says coaches and student-athletes join the group at Kingsbarns on 15 October.
  • October is peak attention on Penn Golf. Three days of national coverage.
  • It was never a signup hook. It is better: a defined, funded, committed group with a fixed date.

07 · First principles

Reading the deal

Strip away the framing of a student asking a favour and look at what each side actually holds.

Ryan holds

  • A working, deployed application built for exactly this problem
  • A 90 year structured roster archive Penn may not itself possess
  • A 4:51 film that makes the case emotionally
  • Insider credibility: a rostered athlete, not a vendor
  • Small but real proof it works, a 15 person tee sheet actually run on it

The Champions Club holds

  • Contactable alumni, with consent and legitimacy
  • Authority to endorse something so it does not read as spam
  • A funded alumni programme already in motion
  • Reach across roughly 33 varsity programmes
  • Continuity that outlasts any one student

Neither asset produces value alone. Software with no alumni is a demo. An alumni list with no product is a spreadsheet and a LinkedIn search. That is genuine complementarity, and complementarity rather than enthusiasm is what makes a partnership rational for both sides.

The counterintuitive move on the data question

The instinct is to hide it: do not mention that you hold 340 people's names with no relationship to any of them. That instinct is wrong. Lead with it:

"I built the archive from public roster pages. I deliberately have no contact information for these people, and I'm not going to go around Penn to get it. That's exactly why I'm here."

Three reasons. They will work it out anyway, and a hesitant answer is fatal. It converts the liability into the reason for the meeting. And it signals judgment, which is the actual thing being assessed when an undergraduate is in that room.

On sequencing

The earlier logic was "build traction first, pitch second." Section 04 substantially weakens that: Ryan cannot manufacture meaningful traction without the channel he would be asking for. The bar before a meeting is therefore not this has traction but this is alive. Real people, real upcoming events, no placeholder cards.

10 · Before any meeting

Risks, honestly stated

  1. Monetization conflict, the one I underrated

    The codebase has live Stripe tiers at $10 and $20 a month, plus a parent tier and donations. Zero subscribers today. But proposing to charge Penn alumni monthly, on a list derived from Penn roster data, under a Champions Club partnership, while the Champions Club is soliciting gifts from those same people, is a direct conflict. Cleanest fix: billing off entirely for anything Penn-partnered. A competing ask on the same alumni is the fastest way to lose this.

  2. Data governance

    340 real people, publicly sourced, no consent obtained, held privately. Be able to state exactly what is stored, where it came from, who can see it, and how someone is removed. It must already be true in the product, not aspirational. Then say it first, before being asked.

  3. Ownership ambiguity

    Built alone, owned alone. "Partnership" spans everything from a pilot licence to Penn assuming control. Decide the floor beforehand: you own the platform, Penn gets a pilot licence. Do not negotiate specifics live while flattered to be in the room.

  4. Single-person continuity

    65,548 lines, one graduating student, no second maintainer. Any institution will ask what happens after. Have an answer even if it is "I intend to keep running it, and here is what a handover would look like."

  5. Timing

    46 days is enough, but only if the email goes out in early September. Sent in October it is worthless, because everyone will be in Scotland or recovering from it.

  6. Unproven demand

    Nobody yet knows whether Penn Golf alumni want this. Three accounts is not evidence either way. Claiming product-market fit would be false and trivially punctured, so do not.

11 · Only Ryan can answer these

Open questions

  1. Are you definitely going to Scotland? The entire plan assumes yes. If not, it needs rebuilding around a different anchor.
  2. Do you already know Charlie Carroll? You built a page promoting his trip. If you have corresponded, the email is a follow-up rather than an introduction, and it should read that way.
  3. What is the actual ask? Permission and a channel; a Scotland pilot; or a multi-team deployment. The plan recommends the pilot as the entry point because it is nearly free for Penn to grant. Multi-team is a procurement conversation measured in quarters.
  4. What is your position on charging money? See risk 1. This one has to be settled before you send anything.
  5. What is your ownership floor? See risk 3. Decide it while calm, not while flattered.
  6. Roughly how many alumni registered for Scotland? Determines whether this is a 10 person or a 40 person pilot. Charlie knows and you may not. It is a reasonable thing to ask in the first reply.

12 · Argue with this

Where I am most likely wrong

  • The pilot may be too small to matter. If only eight alumni registered, success proves little and burns your one warm contact.
  • Charlie may be the wrong function. Champions Club is fundraising-led. Alumni engagement may sit with central Penn Alumni Relations, and a development officer could see this as a distraction from soliciting gifts.
  • The timing may be actively bad. Six weeks out is when a trip organizer is most overloaded. November might get more attention, at the cost of missing the event entirely.
  • Penn may simply decline on risk. An unvetted student-run site touching $10,000 donors is a plausible no regardless of merit.
  • "Only channel" is probably too strong. QuakerNet, class notes, the current roster's parents, one-at-a-time LinkedIn outreach. None scale, but they exist.
  • Zero traction may just be disqualifying, and the right move might be a semester of reaching 50 real users by any means before approaching anyone institutional.
  • The archive's rarity is asserted, not verified. Penn may already hold complete structured roster history.

13 · Shipped 26 August

What changed on the site

Past rounds become records

Four days after the trip, both rounds still showed as Open, counted in the open tally, offered calendar links and exposed live group editing. Gatherings had no past-date logic at all. The root cause was that they stored only a host-typed free-text date, and two parser traps were waiting in it:

InputJavaScript reads it as
"Saturday, June 14"2001-06-14
"Alumni Weekend, 2026"2026-01-01

The first was the host form's own placeholder text, so following the form's example produced a date 25 years in the past, which would sort a brand new round to the top of the board and then bury it as already played. The second grabs a year and ignores everything else. Both are fixed, both host forms now capture a real date, past rounds move to a read-only Recently Played section, and 24 tests cover it. Verified live: the board went from 3 OPEN to 1 OPEN and 2 PLAYED.

Team updates in twenty seconds

The date is now a picker starting on today with Today, Yesterday and Last Sat shortcuts, with a one-tap escape to free text for labels like Championship Weekend. Up to 12 photos or videos, drag to reorder, rendering as a gallery in The Season. The link preview upload stays hidden until there is a link.
