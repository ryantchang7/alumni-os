# Penn Athletics outreach

Drafts for showing Penn Athletics the Clubhouse. Order matters: **coach first.**
A cold email to an Associate AD from a student-athlete usually goes nowhere; the
same email forwarded by your coach gets read.

Links to include:
- Film: https://www.penngolfclubhouse.com/launch
- Site: https://www.penngolfclubhouse.com

Fill in: `[year]` (junior/senior), `[Coach]`, `[Name]`, your phone.

---

## 1. Coach first — text or short email

> Coach — quick one, not golf related.
>
> Over the past year I built a site for Penn Golf alumni: penngolfclubhouse.com.
> Alumni can claim their spot on the roster going back decades, see where old
> teammates ended up, and set up rounds. We used it to run the tee sheet for the
> Boston trip last weekend — 15 guys, four groups, all on the site.
>
> I think Athletics could use it for more than golf. Right now they're doing this
> by hand on LinkedIn. Would you be up for introducing me to whoever runs alumni
> engagement over there? Happy to walk you through it first.

Why this works: it's short, it's already real, and it asks for one specific thing.

---

## 2. The email to Athletics (after the intro)

**Subject options**
- `Penn Golf alumni site — built by a current player`
- `Intro from [Coach] — Penn Golf alumni site`
- `Something I built for Penn Golf alumni`

**Body**

> Hi [Name],
>
> I'm Ryan Chang, [year] on the men's golf team. [Coach] suggested I get in touch.
>
> Over the past year I built penngolfclubhouse.com, a private site for Penn Golf
> alumni and current players. Alumni claim their spot on the roster going back
> decades, see where former teammates ended up, and organize rounds with each
> other. Our team used it to run the tee sheet for the preseason trip in Boston
> last weekend — 15 players across four groups.
>
> I built it because there was no way for a Penn golfer from 1994 and one from
> 2026 to find each other. My understanding is Athletics is doing a version of
> this by hand through LinkedIn.
>
> Two things I'd like to ask:
>
> 1. Twenty minutes to show you the site and a short film I made about it.
> 2. Whether there's a way to reach Penn golf alumni through official channels.
>    That's the one piece I can't do on my own.
>
> If it works for golf, the same thing works for any team.
>
> Film: https://www.penngolfclubhouse.com/launch
> Site: https://www.penngolfclubhouse.com
>
> Thanks for your time,
> Ryan Chang
> rtchang@upenn.edu · [phone]

**Notes on why it reads this way**
- No "platform", "network effects", "engagement". Athletics staff hear that
  pitch constantly from vendors; you are not a vendor.
- The trip detail is the whole email. It's the only sentence proving people
  actually used it.
- The ask is 20 minutes, not a partnership. Partnerships come from the meeting.
- One line about other teams. Enough to plant it, not enough to oversell.

---

## 3. Have these two answers ready before the meeting

**"Where did the alumni data come from?"** — the question most likely to end it.
Answer plainly and first, before being asked:

> The roster history is public information from pennathletics.com. Nobody appears
> as a member until they claim their own spot and approve what's shown. Anyone can
> ask to be removed and I take them down the same day. Nothing is sold, shared, or
> used for anything but the site.

If any of that isn't exactly true today, make it true before the meeting.

**"Who owns it?"** — decide before you're in the room, not during:

> I built and own the site. I'd want Penn Athletics to have it as a pilot for golf,
> and I'm open to what it looks like if it goes beyond one team.

Don't negotiate specifics live. "Let me think about that and come back to you" is
a complete sentence.

---

## 4. If no intro comes in two weeks

Send it cold, same body, minus the `[Coach] suggested` line, plus an opener:

> I'm a [year] on the men's golf team. I built something for our alumni over the
> past year and I think it's relevant to what Athletics is trying to do.

Find the name in the staff directory at pennathletics.com — look for Development,
Advancement, or Alumni Relations. Send to one person, not a list.
